Photo Guide

The better your photo, the more magical your portrait. Here's exactly what works — and what doesn't.

✓ What works
✓
Photo taken at eye level with your pet — crouch down to their world.
✓
Well lit — natural daylight is best. Pro tip: sit your pet in front of a window. Use a chair if you need to get them up to the light.
✓
Close-up portrait with all features visible — head and chest is ideal. Not too far away, but not so close you lose the ears.
✓
Sharp and in focus — you should be able to see individual hairs and whiskers clearly.
✓
An original photo, not a screenshot of another photo or a scan.
✓
A headshot — full body isn't necessary and actually gives less detail in the final portrait.
✗ What doesn't work
✗
Taken from too far away — your pet should fill a good portion of the frame, not be a tiny figure in the corner.
✗
Face obstructed — a hand, lead, toy, or another animal in front of the face will confuse the background removal.
✗
Too dark — low light is the most common cause of poor results, especially with dark-coloured pets.
✗
Blurry or out of focus — motion blur from a wriggly pet doesn't sharpen up well. Wait for a still moment!
✗
Very low resolution — small, pixelated photos will produce a soft, low-detail portrait.
✗
Wrong angle — photos taken from directly above or below are tricky for background removal and rarely look their best.
✗
Eyes closed or unflattering close-up — your pet deserves their best angle! If they look sleepy, try again when they're alert.
